Image-Based Gender Classification: Late Fusion and Knowledge Distillation

Vladimir Ivanovski

Index: 249024

May 2026

Applied ML coursework

Abstract

This project develops an image-based gender classification pipeline for TikTok creator videos requiring no human annotations. Starting from pre-extracted video frames, we apply MTCNN face detection and a ViT baseline model (dima806/man_woman_face_image_detection, 98.7% benchmark accuracy) to generate pseudo-labels. Two progressive improvements are evaluated:

Knowledge Distillation (KD) fine-tunes the model using soft probability targets from the teacher (temperature T=3) combined with a stricter confidence threshold (0.90) for cleaner pseudo-labels. Inference uses late fusion — summing softmax probabilities across all 4 frames per video instead of per-frame majority vote. This achieves 97.2% accuracy — a +5.7% improvement over the v1 baseline (91.5%) and a +9.3% improvement over naive hard-label fine-tuning (87.9%). The results demonstrate that inference aggregation strategy and label quality are more impactful than simply applying fine-tuning.

1. Introduction

Gender classification from video content is a core building block for personalized recommendation systems. This work applies it to TikTok creator videos collected as part of a broader platform using CLIP embeddings, Elasticsearch, Ray distributed computing, and Flask.

The central challenge is the absence of ground-truth labels. We use pseudo-labeling: a high-accuracy pre-trained ViT model generates confident predictions which serve as training signals. However, pseudo-labeling introduces noise, and naive hard-label fine-tuning degrades performance when the teacher model is already near-optimal. This paper introduces two improvements that address this limitation through better inference aggregation and better training signal quality.

3. Methodology

The pipeline runs in two stages. Stage 1 (v1) generates baseline predictions and hard-label fine-tuning. Stage 2 (v2) improves inference and fine-tuning using two techniques.

3.1 Baseline Inference and MTCNN Face Detection (shared)

MTCNN detects the largest face per frame (min confidence 0.90, 20px margin). The 224×224 crop is classified by dima806/man_woman_face_image_detection (ViT-base/16, 98.7% benchmark). Results are saved as per-frame predictions with confidence scores.

3.2 Technique 1 — Late Fusion NEW v2

In v1, video-level predictions used per-frame majority vote with confidence tiebreaking. Late Fusion replaces this with probability aggregation:

  1. For each frame, reconstruct the full softmax distribution [prob_female, prob_male] from the stored (label, confidence) pair.
  2. Sum the probability vectors across all frames of a video.
  3. Take argmax of the summed vector as the video-level prediction.

This ensures that a high-confidence frame (female: 0.99) dominates a low-confidence frame (male: 0.71) rather than each casting an equal vote. No training is required.

3.3 Technique 2 — Knowledge Distillation with Soft Labels NEW v2

Instead of hard 0/1 labels, the teacher's softmax probabilities are used as soft targets with KL divergence loss at temperature T=3.0:

L = KL( softmax(logits/T) || softmax(log(teacher_probs)/T) ) × T²

Temperature scaling softens both distributions, making the student learn the teacher's uncertainty pattern (e.g., a borderline case at 0.62/0.38 is treated differently than a confident 0.97/0.03). Additionally, the training threshold is raised to 0.90 for cleaner pseudo-labels, and late fusion is used at evaluation time.

5. Discussion

Why did v1 hard-label fine-tuning degrade accuracy (-3.6%)? The baseline model is already 98.7% accurate on clean faces. Pseudo-labels at threshold 0.70 contain some borderline predictions. Training on these noisy hard labels pushes the model away from its well-calibrated weights. With only 563 samples, there is not enough signal to overcome the noise.

Why does KD fine-tuning achieve +5.7%? Two factors work together. First, raising the threshold to 0.90 removes the most uncertain pseudo-labels, giving cleaner training data (666 vs 704 videos, but higher average quality). Second, soft labels encode the teacher's uncertainty: a borderline prediction of [0.62, 0.38] becomes a soft target [0.55, 0.45] at T=3, regularising the student instead of forcing it to commit to a hard 0/1 target it may be wrong about. Combined with late fusion at inference, errors drop from 12 to 4 on the 141-video test set.

Audio vs image comparison: The best audio-based result was 86.3% accuracy. The KD image-based result is 97.2% — an 10.9 percentage point improvement. Visual appearance is a substantially stronger signal for gender classification in TikTok creator content.

Key finding: Knowledge distillation with soft labels (KL divergence, T=3) combined with late fusion at inference improves fine-tuning from 87.9% to 97.2% — a +9.3% gain over naive hard-label fine-tuning and +5.7% over the pre-trained baseline.

6. Conclusion

This project demonstrates that combining two ML techniques yields a substantial improvement over the baseline:

  1. Late Fusion at inference: Aggregating softmax probabilities across all 4 video frames — instead of majority vote — uses the full probability distribution and eliminates tie-breaking ambiguity. This alone accounts for a significant share of the gain.
  2. Knowledge Distillation fine-tuning: Replacing hard pseudo-labels with the teacher's soft probability targets (KL divergence, T=3) and using a stricter threshold (0.90) for cleaner training data. This improves fine-tuning from 87.9% (hard labels) to 97.2% (soft labels) — a +9.3% gain over naive hard-label fine-tuning and a +5.7% gain over the v1 baseline.

The results confirm that inference strategy and label quality are both critical. Naive fine-tuning without these improvements actually degrades performance by -3.6%. The final KD+Late Fusion pipeline achieves 97.2% accuracy on the 141-video held-out test set.
