Technique 1 — Late Fusion

Why majority vote fails

Example: 4 frames for one video:

FramePredConfidence
frame_01female0.72
frame_02female0.71
frame_03male0.97
frame_04male0.95

Majority vote: 2 female, 2 male — tie! Use mean confidence: female=0.715, male=0.96 — male wins. OK here, but mean confidence tiebreaking doesn't scale well with more frames.

Late fusion: sum probabilities

FrameP(female)P(male)
frame_010.720.28
frame_020.710.29
frame_030.030.97
frame_040.050.95
SUM1.512.49

argmax = male. Correct! High-confidence frames dominate.

No training needed. Just change how you aggregate frame predictions. High-confidence frames automatically dominate.

Technique 2 — Knowledge Distillation

Why hard labels fail

Hard label training treats every sample equally:

VideoTeacher saysHard label
vid_Afemale: 0.99[1, 0]
vid_Bfemale: 0.62[1, 0]

vid_B is likely a noisy label (62% confidence). The model treats it the same as a 99% confident one — it learns wrong things from borderline cases.

Soft labels fix this

VideoTeacher saysSoft label (T=3)
vid_Afemale: 0.99[0.94, 0.06]
vid_Bfemale: 0.62[0.55, 0.45]

Temperature T=3 softens the distribution. vid_B now has a [0.55, 0.45] target — the student learns "this is uncertain" instead of treating it as a clean label.

L = KL(student_softmax/T || teacher_probs/T) × T²
Key insight (Hinton et al., 2015): The teacher's probability distribution contains more information than just the label. A "91% female, 9% male" prediction teaches the student something about the structure of the problem.

Why KD + Late Fusion Gains +5.7%?

Why Late Fusion helps

The ViT baseline is already 98.7% accurate on clean faces. Errors in v1 (91.5%) come from:

  • Low-quality frames (motion blur, side profile)
  • Majority vote giving equal weight to weak and strong predictions
  • Tie-breaking with mean confidence is fragile

Late fusion fixes this: a 0.99 frame contributes P(female)=0.99, a 0.71 frame contributes 0.71 — high-confidence frames dominate naturally.

Why KD helps over hard-label FT

  • Hard labels treat every sample as equally certain — noisy pseudo-labels corrupt the model
  • Soft targets (T=3) encode uncertainty — a [0.55, 0.45] target says "this is borderline", not "definitely female"
  • Stricter threshold (0.90) filters out the noisiest pseudo-labels before distillation
Result: errors drop from 12 (v1 baseline) to 4 (KD+LF) — a +5.7% accuracy gain and +9.3% over hard-label fine-tuning.

Conclusion

Summary of contributions

1

MTCNN face detection on pre-extracted TikTok frames

2

Automated threshold sweep (balance × √n scoring)

3

Late Fusion: sum softmax probs across frames — high-confidence frames dominate, eliminates tie-breaking fragility

4

Knowledge Distillation: soft labels + KL loss + T=3 → 97.2% (+5.7% vs baseline, +9.3% vs hard FT)

5

Image-based (97.2%) vastly outperforms audio-based (86.3%)

All edge cases handled

Video <4 frames — use however many exist

Frame has no face — skip frame silently

All frames no face — log to skipped_videos.txt

Tie vote → resolved by mean confidence

<10 class samples → skip fine-tuning with warning

No face at training time → black image fallback

Bottom line: Combining late fusion at inference with KD soft-label training yields a +5.7% accuracy gain over the baseline, going from 91.5% to 97.2% on 141 held-out test videos.
